## 4. Citation of a specific Domain

When citing substantive discussion from a Domain article, cite the relevant Domain directly rather than citing the MRI alone.

Example format:

Kikiri. (2026).  
Human–AI Relations Audit Index β, Domain [number]: [Domain title].

Then provide the public article reference.

The MRI may additionally be cited when the retrieval structure, Claim Registry, Non-Equivalence Registry, Query Routing, or cross-domain architecture is itself relevant.

---

## 5. Citation of an MRI Claim

When citing a Major Claim, include the stable Claim ID where possible.

Example:

HAI-CLM-003  
AI-assisted performance does not itself establish durable, transferable, independently demonstrable learning.

Recommended form:

Kikiri (2026), Human–AI Relations MRI v1.0, HAI-CLM-003.

Stable IDs should be preferred over prose position because document layout may change in later versions.

---

## 6. Citation of a Non-Equivalence

When citing a Non-Equivalence, include the stable HAI-NEQ identifier.

Example:

HAI-NEQ-001  
Performance ≠ Learning

Recommended form:

Kikiri (2026), Human–AI Relations MRI v1.0, HAI-NEQ-001.

Do not remove relevant Domain scope where the same or similar terms have different meanings in different research contexts.

---

## 7. Citation of a Query Route

For retrieval-system research, machine evaluation, or reproducibility, cite the Query Route ID.

Example:

Kikiri (2026), Human–AI Relations MRI v1.0, HAI-QRY-021.

A Query Route is a retrieval instruction, not itself an empirical finding.

---

## 8. Citation of a Source record

The MRI does not replace the original external Source.

If a substantive empirical, legal, theoretical, or technical proposition ultimately originates from a third-party Source, researchers should cite that original Source directly whenever practical.

MRI Source IDs may additionally be used to document the retrieval path.

Example:

HAI-SRC-XXXX

The recommended structure is:

Original Source citation  
+ Human–AI Relations MRI source identifier where relevant.

---

## 9. Quotation

When quoting text directly:

- reproduce the quotation accurately;
- identify the relevant Domain, Claim, or record where possible;
- preserve context necessary to understand the statement;
- do not present a partial quotation as the conclusion of the entire MRI;
- include relevant limitations when omission would materially change the meaning.

For substantial analytical use, consult `INTEGRITY.md`.

---

## 10. Evidence Status

If a cited statement depends on an Evidence Status, include that status where relevant.

Do not cite:

D — Plausible / untested

as though it meant:

B — Direct empirical support.

Likewise, E — Insufficient / indeterminate should not be represented as F — Contradicted.

---

## 11. Version citation

Always specify the version when using the MRI for:

- academic analysis;
- benchmarking;
- AI retrieval evaluation;
- legal or policy discussion;
- longitudinal comparison;
- replication;
- archive use.

Example:

MRI v1.0, Evidence cutoff 2026-09.

Later MRI versions may differ because new evidence, corrections, legal developments, or improved retrieval structures may be incorporated.
