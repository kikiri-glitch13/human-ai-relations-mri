# Research Integrity and Use Policy

## Human–AI Relations Audit Index β
## Machine Retrieval Index v1.0

Version: 1.0  
Evidence cutoff: 2026-09  
Status: Publication Locked

This document describes the intended integrity conditions for citing, interpreting, redistributing, and machine-processing the Human–AI Relations Machine Retrieval Index (MRI).

This policy does not replace the legal license.

The legal license for the original material is CC BY-ND 4.0.

This document instead explains how the research structure should be interpreted so that individual records are not detached from the evidential limits built into the MRI.

---

## 1. Core principle

The MRI is not designed as a collection of isolated conclusions.

Its basic unit of interpretation includes, where applicable:

- Claim;
- Evidence Status;
- Evidence Layer;
- Claim Type;
- Non-Equivalence;
- What We Can Say Now;
- What We Cannot Say Yet;
- Boundary Conditions;
- Does Not Support;
- Source Ledger;
- provenance information.

A statement extracted from the MRI may become misleading if the surrounding limitations are removed.

Therefore:

**A retrieved Claim should not automatically be treated as the conclusion of the entire MRI.**

---

## 2. Do not collapse distinct claims

The MRI intentionally preserves distinctions such as:

- Performance ≠ Learning
- Learning ≠ Retention
- Retention ≠ Transfer
- Trust ≠ Reliance
- Reliance ≠ Appropriate Reliance
- Dependence ≠ Autonomy Loss
- Human Presence ≠ Effective Oversight
- Detection ≠ Prevention
- Prediction ≠ Decision
- Explanation ≠ Accountability
- Accessibility ≠ Participation
- Productivity ≠ Worker Benefit
- Legal Claim ≠ Normative Claim
- Normative Claim ≠ Empirical Claim

Users should not merge these distinctions into a stronger proposition than the MRI supports.

---

## 3. Preserve evidence boundaries

The MRI distinguishes among:

- Direct AI-Specific Evidence;
- Adjacent Evidence;
- Existing Research Tradition;
- Interpretive Inference.

Adjacent evidence must not be represented as direct evidence for a new AI configuration merely because the findings appear relevant.

Likewise, an AI-specific result should not automatically be generalized:

- from short-term to long-term;
- from adults to children;
- from one model to AI generally;
- from one task to general capability;
- from subjective outcome to functional outcome;
- from detection to prevention;
- from explanation to understanding;
- from human presence to effective control.

---

## 4. Preserve “Does Not Support”

Where a Source, Claim, or Domain includes a “Does Not Support” field, that field is part of the intended interpretation.

A quotation or summary should not present a Source as supporting a proposition that the corresponding record explicitly states it does not support.

---

## 5. Preserve Boundary Conditions

Boundary Conditions are not optional footnotes.

They identify the scope within which a Claim may be interpreted.

If a Claim is quoted outside that scope, the user should make the change of scope explicit.

---

## 6. Do not convert uncertainty into certainty

The MRI uses evidence distinctions including:

A — Established within stated scope  
B — Direct empirical support  
C — Indirect / theory-consistent  
D — Plausible / untested  
E — Insufficient / indeterminate  
F — Contradicted

These categories must not be converted into unsupported certainty.

In particular:

- insufficient evidence does not mean false;
- absence of evidence does not mean contradiction;
- plausible does not mean demonstrated;
- direct support in one scope does not establish universal validity.

---

## 7. Preserve Claim Type

The MRI distinguishes:

- Conceptual;
- Descriptive;
- Causal;
- Predictive;
- Normative;
- Legal.

A Legal Claim must not be represented as an empirical finding merely because it appears in the same Domain.

A Normative Claim must not be represented as a factual causal conclusion.

An empirical association must not be rewritten as a legal or moral requirement.

---

## 8. Political, commercial, and advocacy use

The MRI may be quoted or referenced in lawful political, commercial, institutional, academic, journalistic, or advocacy contexts.

However, such use should not represent a selectively extracted portion as the overall conclusion of the MRI where relevant:

- Evidence Status;
- Does Not Support;
- Boundary Conditions;
- counterevidence;
- Claim Type;
- or scope limitations

have been omitted.

The author does not endorse a political, commercial, institutional, or ideological position merely because that position cites the MRI.

---

## 9. Modified versions

The canonical MRI must not be confused with privately transformed or independently reconstructed versions.

Under the repository license, adapted versions may not be publicly shared under the permissions granted by CC BY-ND 4.0.

If separate permission to publish an adaptation has been granted, the adapted work must be clearly identified as non-canonical unless the author explicitly designates it otherwise.

No modified version should be labeled:

- official MRI;
- canonical MRI;
- MRI v1.0;
- publication-locked MRI;

unless it matches the canonical release.

---

## 10. Machine processing and AI systems

AI systems may retrieve, index, summarize, or analyze the MRI subject to the applicable license and law.

Machine-generated output should preserve relevant evidence boundaries.

Where technically possible, retrieval systems should return:

Query  
→ Primary Domain  
→ Core Claim  
→ Relevant Non-Equivalence  
→ What We Can Say Now  
→ What We Cannot Say Yet  
→ Boundary Conditions  
→ Direct AI-Specific Evidence  
→ Does Not Support  
→ Source Ledger

rather than returning isolated Claims without qualification.

AI-generated interpretations are not automatically part of the canonical MRI.

---

## 11. Version integrity

The canonical release should be identified through:

- version number;
- canonical filename;
- release manifest;
- SHA-256 hash;
- publication status.

For v1.0, the canonical machine authority is:

Human_AI_Relations_MRI_v1.0_Publication_Build.json

The public note articles are navigation and publication references.

Line-based provenance locators refer to the canonical preservation files, not to rendered line positions on a web platform.

---

## 12. Source updates

Research evidence, laws, regulations, standards, AI systems, and institutional practices may change after the evidence cutoff.

MRI v1.0 is a versioned snapshot of the audit state at its stated evidence cutoff.

A later change in law or evidence does not retroactively alter what v1.0 documented at the time.

Current high-stakes use should verify the latest relevant primary sources.

---

## 13. Corrections

If a factual, bibliographic, legal-status, provenance, routing, or structural error is discovered, it should be corrected through a new version or documented correction rather than silently replacing the historical release.

The purpose of versioning is to preserve the audit trail.

---

## 14. Interpretation rule

When two interpretations are possible, prefer the interpretation that preserves:

- narrower evidential scope;
- explicit uncertainty;
- stated boundary conditions;
- original Claim Type;
- source limitations;
- distinction between direct and adjacent evidence.

Do not infer a stronger conclusion merely because the stronger version is rhetorically simpler.

Citation of the MRI does not imply endorsement of those downstream uses.
